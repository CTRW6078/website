How a part gets made

Every custom part on the robot goes through the same four operations. This is our routing sheet, the same kind of traveler a machine shop sends along with a job.

OpOperationWork centerPhoto
  1. OP 10

    Design

    Every part starts as a CAD model in Onshape. We check fit and clearance in the full robot assembly before anything gets cut.

    Work centerCAD workstation
  2. OP 20

    Cut and bend

    We get our custom plates and brackets cut from sheet aluminum by Fabworks, then bent and tapped. Sending out the cutting gives us back build time for assembly and driver practice.

    Work centerFabworks
  3. OP 30

    Assemble and wire

    Students assemble every subsystem and build the wiring harness by hand, crimping and routing each connection.

    Work centerAssembly bench
  4. OP 40

    Test and drive

    The robot goes on the practice field as early as we can get it there. Driver practice finds problems that CAD can’t, and we tune our autonomous paths with Pedro Pathing.

    Work centerPractice field

Who we are

Cut the Red Wire is a FIRST Tech Challenge team from Apex, North Carolina. We have competed every season since 2012.

We build out of Apex Maker Club, a nonprofit maker space in Apex. Our ten students split into programming, CAD, mechanical, electrical and outreach subteams, each with a student lead.

Outreach

We want more kids in our community to get their hands on a robot, so we teach STEM through a summer camp, workshops and demos.

MetLife STEM Camp

Every year we host a week-long STEM camp for 20 campers. Our members teach engineering, coding and robotics hands-on, with the team’s robots on a full field.

Community STEM workshops

We bring robots to local schools and community centers and run hands-on demos and activities for younger students.

A visit from Wiley Nickel

In February 2025, former U.S. Representative Wiley Nickel met with the team to hear about our STEM work and our plans for a robotics camp for underprivileged youth in North Carolina.
