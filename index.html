Rift · 2025–26

Our robot for DECODE, the 2025–26 FIRST Tech Challenge game. Its signature mechanism is a clutch power take-off that shifts drive power to the endgame climb. Select a numbered balloon to find its subsystem in the list.

Rift subsystems
Item Subsystem Notes
1 Chassis Pocketed side plates, cut by Fabworks
2 Drivetrain and PTO climb A clutch PTO shifts drive power to the endgame climb
3 Intake Multi-roller intake for artifacts
4 Launcher Scores artifacts into the goal
5 Vision Limelight camera on the top mast

Designed in Onshape · autonomous paths with Pedro Pathing · plates by Fabworks

Who we are

Cut the Red Wire is a FIRST Tech Challenge team from Apex, North Carolina. We have competed every season since 2012.

We build out of Apex Maker Club, a nonprofit maker space in Apex. Our ten students split into programming, CAD, mechanical, electrical and outreach subteams, each with a student lead.

Outreach

We want more kids to get their hands on a robot, especially kids who don’t usually get the chance. We run camps and workshops, and work with local leaders to open up more STEM opportunities for underprivileged youth.

MetLife STEM Camp

Every year we host a week-long STEM camp for 20 campers. Our members teach engineering, coding and robotics hands-on, with the team’s robots on a full field.

Purpose Learning Lab camps

We run hands-on robotics sessions for underprivileged youth at Purpose Learning Lab’s camps.

Growing the partnership

We meet with Purpose Learning Lab to plan more STEM opportunities for the kids at their camps.

Working with community leaders

Senator Ted Budd’s office

We brought Rift to Senator Ted Budd’s office and met with his staff about creating STEM opportunities for underprivileged youth.

Steve Rao at Best Brains

We demoed our robot for Steve Rao and a room of students at Best Brains, and talked about bringing STEM to more kids at camps like Purpose Learning Lab.

Former Rep. Wiley Nickel

Former U.S. Representative Wiley Nickel met with the team to hear about our STEM work and our plans for a robotics camp for underprivileged youth.
